Methodology

This page explains the formulas and tax-rule layers used across US Salary Tools. Last reviewed: August 27, 2026.

How the paycheck engine works

The shared engine follows this order: gross annual wages → pre-tax deductions → federal taxable income and withholding → FICA → state taxable income and state income tax → supported state payroll taxes → supported local tax → post-tax deductions → net pay. Annual tax liability and paycheck withholding are kept as separate concepts.

Federal income tax

Federal annual tax uses the 2026 IRS marginal brackets and applicable standard deduction. Paycheck withholding uses the 2026 IRS Percentage Method schedules in the withholding data module. W-4 Step 2, Step 3, Step 4(a), Step 4(b), and Step 4(c) inputs are kept separate from annual tax liability.

FICA

Social Security is calculated at 6.2% up to the 2026 wage base. Medicare is 1.45% on Medicare wages, with Additional Medicare Tax withholding rules applied separately where applicable.

State income tax

State income tax is not one flat rate per state anymore. Where state data is modeled as progressive, the engine walks the state's published brackets. Flat-tax states use their flat rate. State standard deductions/exemptions are applied from the state data module before the bracket calculation.

State payroll taxes

Employee state payroll deductions are modeled separately from state income tax. The state data layer currently includes verified examples such as California SDI and additional 2026 rules for New York Paid Family Leave, Washington Paid Family & Medical Leave, and WA Cares. These amounts are shown separately in the calculators so they are not hidden inside the state-income-tax line.

Supported local taxes

Local income tax is now a separate layer. The current verified local rules module supports New York City, Philadelphia, and Detroit, with resident/nonresident treatment where applicable. A local jurisdiction must be selected; unsupported city/county taxes are intentionally left unmodeled rather than guessed.

Pay frequency

The engine supports annual, monthly, semimonthly, biweekly, weekly, and daily period allocation. The Paycheck and State Comparison interfaces currently expose weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, and monthly choices.

Deductions

Traditional 401(k), HSA, and health-premium inputs are classified as pre-tax according to the state/federal treatment available in the data layer. Post-tax deductions are kept separate and reduce net pay without reducing taxable wages.

2026 worker deductions

The federal special-deduction layer supports qualifying 2026 deductions such as qualified overtime, qualified tips, senior deduction eligibility, and qualified car-loan interest, subject to the limits and phaseouts stored in the federal data module.

What remains intentionally unmodeled

  • Many Ohio municipal, Indiana county, Michigan city, Pennsylvania municipal, Maryland county, Kentucky occupational, and other location-specific taxes.
  • ZIP/address-level local tax lookup.
  • Every state-specific credit, itemized deduction, retirement-income exception, and special filing rule.
  • Employer-specific payroll setup, benefits, and withholding elections not represented by the inputs.
Important: “Modeled” means the rule is represented in the engine; it does not mean a payroll guarantee. Unsupported local jurisdictions are explicitly flagged as estimates rather than silently treated as zero.

Sources

Federal: IRS Publication 15-T. Social Security: SSA wage-base data. California SDI: California EDD. New York Paid Family Leave: NYS PFL. Washington PFML: Washington Paid Leave. Philadelphia: City of Philadelphia. Detroit: City of Detroit.
