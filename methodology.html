Methodology

Salary, Hourly-to-Salary & Salary-to-Hourly Calculators

These three calculators share the same underlying math:

FigureFormula
Annual (from hourly)Hourly rate × hours/week × weeks/year
Hourly (from annual)Annual ÷ (hours/week × weeks/year)
MonthlyAnnual ÷ 12
BiweeklyAnnual ÷ 26
WeeklyAnnual ÷ 52
DailyWeekly ÷ 5

Monthly, biweekly, and weekly figures assume a standard year-round pay schedule (52 weeks). The hourly-equivalent figure uses your actual entered hours per week and weeks per year, so it stays accurate for part-year or part-time schedules.

Overtime Calculator

Regular PayRegular hourly rate × regular hours
Overtime RateRegular hourly rate × overtime multiplier
Overtime PayOvertime rate × overtime hours
Total PayRegular pay + overtime pay

The default multiplier is 1.5x ("time and a half"), the U.S. federal standard for eligible overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This calculator does not determine your overtime eligibility — it performs the math once you supply your hours and rate.

Raise Calculator

Raise AmountCurrent salary × (raise % ÷ 100)
New SalaryCurrent salary + raise amount
Monthly / Biweekly IncreaseRaise amount ÷ 12, or ÷ 26
Hourly IncreaseRaise amount ÷ (hours/week × weeks/year)

2. FICA (Social Security & Medicare)

Applied to gross wages, independent of filing status brackets:

Social Security6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500
Medicare1.45% of all wages, no cap
Additional Medicare Tax+0.9% on wages above $200,000 ($250,000 if Married Filing Jointly; $125,000 if Married Filing Separately)

3. State income tax (simplified)

Modeling all 50 states' exact bracket structures, standard deductions, and local taxes is beyond what a free, transparent calculator can responsibly claim to do. Instead, we apply one flat approximate rate per state directly to your gross salary. This is a deliberate simplification — treat it as directionally useful, not exact.

What the Paycheck Calculator does NOT include

  • Pre-tax deductions: 401(k)/403(b) contributions, HSA/FSA, employer health insurance premiums
  • Tax credits: Child Tax Credit, Earned Income Tax Credit, education credits, etc.
  • Local or city income taxes (e.g., NYC, Philadelphia)
  • Self-employment tax, additional income, or itemized deductions

Sources

Federal bracket and standard deduction figures are drawn from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 as adjusted by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), cross-referenced against third-party tax policy research. FICA figures are drawn from Social Security Administration published wage base figures. State rates are our own simplified approximations compiled from publicly available state tax agency information. This page is reviewed periodically — always confirm critical figures against irs.gov or ssa.gov directly.
