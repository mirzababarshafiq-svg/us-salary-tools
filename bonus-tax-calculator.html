2026 Federal Income Tax, FICA & Sample Pay Estimates

Understanding your take-home pay requires a close look at the 2026 tax tables and withholding rules. When you earn a salary, your gross pay is subject to multiple layers of taxation, primarily federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare (collectively known as FICA). Depending on the state you live in, you might also be subject to state and local income taxes. For the 2026 tax year, the IRS has implemented inflation adjustments to the standard deduction and tax brackets under recent legislation.

The standard deduction is a specific dollar amount that reduces your taxable income. For 2026, the standard deduction for single filers is $16,100, meaning the first $16,100 of your income is not subject to federal income tax. For married couples filing jointly, the standard deduction is $32,200, and for heads of household, it's $24,150.

2026 Federal Income Tax Brackets (Single Filers)

The United States uses a progressive tax system, which means your income is divided into chunks, or "brackets," and each chunk is taxed at a specific rate. You do not pay the highest rate on all of your income; rather, you only pay the highest rate on the portion of your income that falls into that specific bracket. Below are the 2026 federal income tax brackets for single filers.

2026 FICA Withholding Limits

FICA taxes fund Social Security and Medicare. Both employees and employers pay FICA taxes. The Social Security tax is 6.2% on earnings up to the annual wage base limit, which is $184,500 for 2026. The Medicare tax is 1.5% on all earnings, with an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% applying to earnings above $200,000 for single filers.

Tax Type Employee Rate 2026 Wage Limit
