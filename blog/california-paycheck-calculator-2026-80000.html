California Paycheck Calculator 2026: What Is $80,000 After Taxes?

An $80,000 salary sounds straightforward until payday arrives. Federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, California income tax, and California State Disability Insurance can all reduce what reaches your bank account.

Quick answer: under the current US Salary Tools planning model for a single filer with no extra deductions, $80,000 in California is roughly $60,700 a year after estimated taxes, or about $5,059 a month and $2,335 biweekly. This is an estimate, not a guaranteed payroll result.

How much is $80,000 after taxes in California in 2026?

The exact result depends on filing status, benefits, retirement contributions, tax elections, and other personal circumstances. The simple example assumes single filing status, standard deductions, no dependents, no 401(k), HSA, or health-premium inputs, and no extra local income tax.

Why the number is lower than $80,000

Your paycheck is reduced by several separate systems. Federal income tax is calculated using federal taxable income and marginal brackets. California uses a progressive income-tax structure rather than one flat rate. FICA covers Social Security and Medicare. California SDI is a separate employee payroll withholding.

California SDI

California EDD lists a 2026 SDI withholding rate of 1.3%. At $80,000 of wages, a simple 1.3% calculation is about $1,040 for the year, before payroll-specific adjustments.

California income tax

California is progressive, so the rate on your highest slice of taxable income is not applied to the entire salary. That is why using a headline percentage for the whole $80,000 can produce a misleading answer.

Federal income tax

For a single filer, the IRS lists a 2026 federal standard deduction of $16,100. The remaining taxable income moves through the federal marginal brackets rather than being taxed at one single percentage.

What can change your take-home pay?

Filing status: Single, Married Filing Jointly, and Head of Household can produce different results.

401(k): A traditional 401(k) contribution can reduce taxable income while moving money into retirement savings.

Health insurance: Pre-tax premiums can change taxable wages.

HSA: Eligible HSA contributions can affect taxable income depending on how they are made and your coverage.

Credits and dependents: A simple salary-only estimate does not model every personal credit or household situation.

California vs. Texas or Florida

A no-income-tax state can look attractive in a salary comparison, but take-home pay is only one part of a job decision. Housing, insurance, commuting, and other living costs can easily outweigh a tax difference.
