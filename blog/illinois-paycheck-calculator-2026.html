Illinois Paycheck Calculator 2026 — How Much Is $80K a Year After Taxes?

A single filer earning $80,000 in Illinois in 2026 keeps an estimated $61,294.79 in this calculator. Illinois does have a wage income tax. It is a flat 4.95%, not a set of brackets, and it is not 4.95% of the whole salary. The state first subtracts a $2,925 personal exemption. What remains, $77,075, is taxed at 4.95%, which is $3,815.21.

That is the Illinois paycheck in one sentence, and it is the part a “flat tax” headline skips. Chicago does not add a city wage tax on top. Texas and Florida add no state income tax at all. Illinois sits between those two stories.

Quick answer: On $80,000 gross, single, no 401(k), HSA, or health premiums, the 2026 US Salary Tools estimate for Illinois is $61,294.79 of annual take-home pay. The same $80,000 in Texas or Florida comes to $65,110.00. The $3,815.21 gap is Illinois income tax, not a payroll add-on.

Searches like “Illinois paycheck calculator” or “$80,000 after taxes in Illinois” return an estimate, not a stub. This page models one scenario: tax year 2026, gross $80,000.00, single, under 65, not blind, not claimed as someone else’s dependent, no local wage tax, no pre-tax benefits. It is a US Salary Tools engine output — not an Illinois Department of Revenue determination.

How the 4.95% is actually applied

Illinois does not use the federal standard deduction on the state return, and it does not walk 10% / 12% / 22% brackets. One rate, after an exemption.

2026 Illinois income tax walk for $80,000 single
StepAmount
Gross wages in this example$80,000.00
Personal exemption$2,925
Illinois net income taxed here$77,075
4.95% × $77,075$3,815.21

Skipping the exemption and multiplying $80,000 × 4.95% produces $3,960.00, which is $144.79 too high for this filer. This engine uses $3,815.21.

The Illinois Department of Revenue sets the 2026 basic exemption at $2,925 per person, up from $2,850 in 2025. Two limits matter, and this example clears both:

  • The exemption is not allowed when federal AGI is over $250,000 ($500,000 if married filing jointly). An $80,000 salary is under that line.
  • If another person can claim you as a dependent and your Illinois base income is over $2,925, the allowance can be $0. This scenario assumes you are not someone else’s dependent.
  • Age 65 or legal blindness adds $1,000 more. This scenario assumes neither.

Chicago is not a second income tax

New York City residents pay a city income tax on top of New York State. Chicago does not do that on wages. In this engine, local income tax for Illinois is $0.00 whether the job is in Chicago, Springfield, or Rockford.

That does not make Chicago cheap. Sales tax and property tax are real, and they are not paycheck lines. Use take-home as one input, not the whole offer.

Why your actual Illinois paycheck will differ

  • Exemption phaseout. Federal AGI over $250,000 ($500,000 joint) loses the Illinois exemption. This $80,000 example still gets it.
  • Age 65 or blindness. Each adds $1,000 to the exemption. Not included here.
  • Dependent status. If someone else can claim you, the allowance can drop to $0.
  • Pre-tax benefits. A traditional 401(k), HSA, or pre-tax health premium lowers federal taxable wages. Illinois also conforms to those pre-tax amounts in this engine. This scenario uses none.
  • W-4 withholding is not the same as the annual federal bill of $8,770.00.
  • Border jobs. Working in one state and living in another can change which state withholds. This page assumes Illinois wages taxed by Illinois.
  • Overtime. See the no tax on overtime 2026 guide if overtime is a regular part of pay.
