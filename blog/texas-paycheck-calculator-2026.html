Texas Paycheck Calculator 2026 — How Much Is $80K a Year After Taxes?

You earn $80,000 a year in Texas. Unlike many states, Texas does not have an individual personal income tax, so your employee paycheck does not include a Texas state personal income-tax deduction.

That does not mean the entire $80,000 reaches your bank account. Federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare still apply, and your real paycheck can also change because of benefits, retirement contributions, withholding choices, and other payroll settings.

Quick answer: For a single employee earning $80,000 with no voluntary pre-tax deductions, the 2026 US Salary Tools estimate is $65,110.00 annual take-home pay, or $5,425.83 per month.

What is the federal taxable income on $80,000?

For the single-filer example, the federal standard deduction is $16,100. That leaves $63,900 of federal taxable income before other adjustments in this simplified example.

The estimated federal income tax is $8,770.00. That is about 11.0% of gross salary for the federal income-tax line alone.

Texas has no individual state income tax

The biggest Texas paycheck difference is simple: there is no Texas individual personal income tax in this employee estimate.

So, unlike a state with an individual income-tax system, the $80,000 Texas example has $0.00 of Texas state income tax. It also has $0.00 of modeled Texas state payroll tax in this example.

This does not remove federal taxes. Social Security and Medicare still come out of a typical employee paycheck.

Social Security and Medicare still apply

Texas does not replace federal payroll taxes. In this $80,000 example, Social Security is $4,960.00 and Medicare is $1,160.00, for a combined $6,120.00.

That means the Texas example still has substantial payroll deductions even without a state personal income tax.

Texas personal income tax vs. business franchise tax

It is important not to mix up an employee's paycheck with taxes that can apply to businesses.

The Texas franchise tax is a business tax. It is not a personal income-tax deduction applied to an employee's ordinary wages in the paycheck example on this page.

Does Texas have a city wage tax like New York City?

Texas does not have a New York City-style personal city income-tax system that is deducted from an ordinary Texas employee's paycheck in this estimate. That means there is no NYC-style local personal income-tax line being added to the $80,000 Texas example.

Local property taxes, sales taxes, and other taxes can still matter to your overall cost of living, but they are different from a city wage-income tax.

What can change your Texas take-home pay?

Two people can earn the same salary in Texas and receive different paychecks.

401(k) contributions

A traditional 401(k) contribution can reduce federal taxable income while also sending part of your gross pay directly into retirement savings.

HSA and health premiums

Supported HSA contributions and pre-tax health premiums can change taxable income and the cash that reaches your bank account.

W-4 and other withholding choices

Withholding is a prepayment choice, not the same thing as your final tax liability. Extra withholding can make your paycheck smaller even when your underlying annual tax calculation is unchanged.
